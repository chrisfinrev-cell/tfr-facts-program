Why

Budgeting apps make people feel bad.
That's the whole problem.

Every major personal finance app is built on the same flawed premise: track what you spent, then feel guilty about it. The entire category is reactive.

😔
Reactive by design
You spend first. The app tells you the damage afterward. No direction was set — just post-mortem accounting.
📉
Guilt loop
Users check the app, feel bad, avoid the app, fall further behind, feel worse. Churn is a product of the experience model, not the price.
🔄
Zero differentiation
Monarch, YNAB, Copilot, Mint — all tracking tools with different UIs. Nobody has changed the fundamental model.
💡 The insight: People don't fail at budgeting because they lack willpower. They fail because the model demands they track failure instead of directing success. The category calls this normal. We call it an opportunity.

How · Product Flow

Sign up. Allocate. Track. Reach freedom.

The user journey is four steps. Each step builds confidence. The product gets stickier as more data accumulates.

1️⃣
Create account & onboard
Phase Zero captures income sources, account counts, and sets up the full financial profile in under 5 minutes.
2️⃣
Set your allocations
Choose your % split across 6 categories. FACTS calculates dollar amounts from your income automatically.
3️⃣
Track every transaction
Manual entry or Plaid auto-import (Pro). Reports update in real-time. Debt payoff tracked automatically.

Financial Freedom Number + Sunset Visualization shows your path to independence. What-if modeling accelerates the journey.
